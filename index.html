Friedman Economic Games · Proposal 6

Elasticity, TR, MR

Draw a demand curve, then reshape it and watch its elasticity, total revenue and marginal revenue change with it. At an interior maximum of total revenue, elasticity is one and marginal revenue is zero. Explore how a price change affects revenue, compare curves, and test your predictions. An endpoint maximum need not satisfy the interior rule.

How to use it
  1. Start from a preset, or draw your own demand curve: the Line tool takes two clicks, and the Sketch tool follows your pointer with smoothing. You can also type the points into the table below the graph.
  2. With the Reshape tool, drag the square handles, or Tab to one and use the arrow keys. Clicking elsewhere on the graph picks a quantity.
  3. Turn on Elasticity, Total revenue and Marginal revenue to see them drawn from your curve. The aligned panels use the same quantity scale, each with its own units. The original overlay view is also available; its Right axis button switches between elasticity and TR.
  4. Choose a quantity and turn on Show tangent calculation to compute elasticity from the two tangent segments and compare it with the formula.

Exploration checklist

These checks describe the current curve. They are not a score.

    Why do the three views move together?

    TR = P × Q. Along demand, MR = dTR/dQ = P + Q(dP/dQ). Where price, quantity, and slope are nonzero, this is also MR = P(1 − 1/e).

    For a small price cut: elastic demand (e > 1) raises revenue; inelastic demand (e < 1) lowers it. At e = 1 revenue is locally stationary. That condition alone does not prove a maximum on an arbitrary curved demand.

    The shaded rectangle on the demand graph has area P × Q. Follow the same quantity down the other panels to read total revenue and elasticity on their own scales.
